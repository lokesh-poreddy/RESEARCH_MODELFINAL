ChemReact Mathematical Paradigm & Reaction-Kinetics Formulation

1. State Variables & Physical Reaction Vessel Analogies

Software components are treated as a reactive continuous reactor characterized by a 12-dimensional thermodynamic and kinetic state vector:

$$\mathbf{x}_{\text{kinetics}} = \big[\, [C],\, [D],\, [K],\, T_{\text{eff}},\, E_a,\, k_f,\, r_f,\, \Delta G^\ddagger,\, K_{\text{eq}},\, \nabla \mu,\, \bar{\kappa},\, D_{\text{diff}} \,\big]$$
  • $[C]$ (Code Concentration): Active code volume, token density, Halstead volume.
  • $[D]$ (Churn Feed Flux): Commits per sprint, token additions/deletions velocity.
  • $[K]$ (Structural Instability): McCabe cyclomatic complexity, coupling, AST depth.
  • $T_{\text{eff}}$ (Thermal Entropy): Sprint deadline pressure, developer context switching, cognitive noise.

2. Activation Energy Barrier ($E_a$) & Arrhenius Defect Nucleation Rate

Defects require activation energy to overcome defensive software engineering barriers:

$$E_a = E_0 + \sum_{i=1}^{4} \alpha_i Q_i = E_0 + 1.2\,\theta_{\text{cov}} + 0.9\,\theta_{\text{type}} + 1.4\,\theta_{\text{rev}} + 1.0\,\theta_{\text{mod}}$$

The forward kinetic rate constant of defect formation is governed by Arrhenius kinetics:

$$k_f = A \exp\left(-\frac{E_a}{R \cdot T_{\text{eff}}}\right), \quad r_f = k_f \cdot [C]^\alpha \cdot [D]^\beta \cdot [K]^\gamma$$

3. Test-Case Generation as Chemical Affinity Titration

Test cases act as neutralizing reagents with transition-state binding affinity $\kappa_{ij}$:

$$\kappa_{ij} = \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta G^\ddagger(t_i, d_j)}{k_B \cdot T_{\text{eff}}}\right)$$

The Titration Equivalence Point represents full neutralization of active defect sites:

$$\sum_{i=1}^N \kappa_{ij} [T_i] \ge [D_j], \quad \forall j \in \{1, \dots, M\}$$
